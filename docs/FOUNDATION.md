# Foundation agreement

The accepted project is small enough to understand, inspect, and deliberately
change. Its structure can grow through use.

## What belongs now

- Landing page: introduce the overall undertaking. Keep its copy in HTML.
- Journal: compose an encounter with an idea or work. Start with one reset entry.
- Orb: explore the structured material. Start with the four existing Horizons.
- Schema and records: document meaning and validate the current commitments.

These are different levels of presentation, not necessarily three renderings of
identical content. A Journal entry may link to a Horizon without being generated
from it. Layout choices do not redefine record meaning.

## Meaning and identity

A Horizon is a value, guiding principle, long-term vision, or near-term goal.
Its level describes its role, not its importance or distance in time:

1. Values: enduring goods that give the practice its purpose.
2. Guiding principles: ongoing commitments that shape decisions.
3. Long-term vision: a desired future way of life, community, or organization.
4. Near-term goals: concrete outcomes pursued to advance that vision.

Keep permanent IDs and existing creation timestamps. Unknown dates stay unknown.
Record creation is distinct from publication and from the origin of an idea.
Markdown bodies hold elaboration. Introduce a field when a real entry or repeated
use needs it; leave unfinished thinking in prose until its structure is useful.

## A review that maintains trust

Once a week, or before accepting a substantial change:

- Read the active Horizons. Do their meanings and levels still fit?
- Inspect changes since the previous review. Are all changes intentional?
- Check validation and read the rendered pages, including direct links.
- Correct omissions or misleading entries; park ideas that are still uncertain.
- Leave a short dated review note in the PR description or commit message when
  accepting a change. Git records the accepted version; no review database is needed.

Validation checks structure and uniqueness. Human review checks truth, relevance,
and whether the project still expresses what we intend. Neither replaces the other.

## Exploration and acceptance

The baseline tag and lab branch preserve the full earlier project; see README.
The lab may move faster than our understanding. The accepted branch should change
at a pace we can absorb. Bring back one useful idea through a focused feature PR,
including only the records, contracts, code, and checks it actually requires.

Before accepting an addition, explain what it makes possible, why it belongs now,
and how its smallest useful version was checked. A complete lab merge is not the
normal promotion path. Existing code is available to reuse, not an obligation to
restore every prior feature.

Do not expand the schema, build a CMS, or add workflow machinery merely to make
this agreement more elaborate. First use the complete cycle: write, validate,
read, revise, inspect, accept, and revisit.
